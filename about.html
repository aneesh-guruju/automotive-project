About me

Learning Automotive Engineering through Curiosity

I've always been Interested in Automobiles, but my interest in Automotive Engineering grew from wanting to understand how vehicles function beyond their exterior design and their performance specs. Through technical study, workshop exposure, and analytical observation, I've been developing a deeper understanding of automotive systems and engineering decisions.

How It Started

From Admiration to Technical Curiosity

My interest in cars gradually grew from simply appreciating vehicles visually to wanting to understand how they function mechanically. Over time, I became increasingly interested in engine systems, suspension behavior, vehicle dynamics, and the engineering tradeoffs involved in automotive design. This curiosity eventually pushed me toward technical study, workshop exposure, and deeper analytical observation.

Practical Exposure

Learning Beyond Technical Theory

Alongside techincal study, I've also gained exposure to real-world automotive work via observing repairs, and mechanical systems in workshop environments. This exposure helped me better understand how engineering concepts apply beyond theory, especially when dealing with system maintenance, system failures, and real-world usability.

Workshop Experience

Observations from Practical Exposure

One particularly memorable experience involved observing the removal of an engine from a Suzuki Swift after a crankshaft-related failure. Watching the disassembly process provided insight into how interconnected automotive systems actually are, while also highlighting the practical challenges involved in disconnecting the electicals, repair accessibility, and mechanical workflow.

  • Understanding how engine placement affects accessibility and repairs.
  • Observing the components which are connected to the engine.
  • Learning the importance of maintaining the enigne.
  • Gaining exposure to real-world workshop workflow and repair processes.
