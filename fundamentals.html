Engine Systems

The Core Power Unit of a Vehicle

Engines convert fuel into mechanical energy through controlled combustion (The 4-stroke cycle). Beyond performance figures, engine design also affects reliability, cooling efficiency, maintenance accessibility, and fuel economy.

  • Power: Converts fuel to mechanical motion via the four-stroke cycle.
  • Cooling: Manages extreme combustion heat to prevent structural engine failure.
  • Reliability and mechanical stress
  • Packaging and maintenance accessibility

Suspension Systems

Balancing Comfort and Vehicle Control

Suspension systems influence handling, ride comfort, stability, and tire contact with the road surface. Engineers must balance performance, responsiveness, and everyday usability.

  • Weight transfer and chassis balance
  • Ride comfort vs handling tradeoffs
  • Suspension geometry fundamentals
  • Vehicle stability during cornering

Braking Systems

Control, Heat Management, and Safety

Braking systems are critical for vehicle control, stability, and safety. Effective braking depends not only on stopping power, but also on thermal efficiency, tire grip, and brake balance.

  • Heat generation during braking
  • Brake fade and thermal limitations
  • Vehicle stability under braking
  • Brake balance and control
