Case Study

Analyzing Real-World Automotive Problems

This section documents a case-study I've done on a rebuild video of a Porsche. The refrence is from a youtube channel called Matt Armstrong, he does rebuild videos and keeps the video entertaining while providing technical information and procedure on how he rebuilds the cars.

Vehicle Overview

This car has been submerged underwater.

  • The Porsche 911 GT3RS is a track-focused car, it has a 4 litre naturally aspirated Flat 6 engine, producing 518hp and 465nm of torque, RWD, and weighs just 1450 kg. Wessiach package included.
  • Battery and tires damaged.
  • Heavy chasis damage, Possible electrical damages.
  • Assumed no rust since it's been in fresh water..

Problem Analysis

Understanding the Failure

This car has been in an accident, was submerged in water so high chances of rust in components like the engine. Water has gone into alot of vital parts like the engine etc.

  • Been in an accident, hit 2 trees (Caused heavy chasis damage) and fell into a lake (Components flooded with water.)
  • Coil packs wet, spark plug hole filled with water, Cylinders rusted.
  • Hole in the gearbox, broken inlet manifold, oil milky due to water being mixed in it, engine flooded with water too.
  • Porsche has designed the GT3RS very well in this case, all of the oil is stored in one place, making it easier to change it and access it incase of circumstances like this, since the cylinders were rusted, the engine had to be removed, this car was designed for the track, hence the engine was fairly easy to remove.

Diagnosis Process

Observing the Repair Workflow

So they decided to first clean the rust in the cylinders, since every millimeter in the cylinder matters, the piston is put such that it fits perfectly in the cylinder. However, the rust had already damaged the cylinder, so they had to order a new cylinder.

  • For the remaining part, the pistons were fine, they were places at TDC (top dead center) which is the point where the piston is at the top of the cylinder. All of the pistons were perfectly at the TDC confirming there was no bending/damage in the con-rods.
  • To inspect the components inside the engine, they had to completely strip it apart, this helped me better understand how the engine looks and works.
  • During the dis-assembly of the engine, I've observed how the components look and the precision required in making an engine.
  • Matt started by dis-assembling the engine, and replacing the rusted components, analyzing the damaged parts, and asssembling it again using the proper procedure. All while the chasis was being re-built.

Engineering Observations

Design Decisions and Tradeoffs

This Porsche is a track car, and is designed for parts to be easily accessible. However, they placede the power fuse box right under the dash board, making it hard to access.

  • Now since it was a track based car, the engine and transmission were fairly simple to remove and access, although the crash did most of the part for it.
  • The fuel tank in this car however, was placed at the front of the car.
  • This car also has its own DRS (Drag reduction system) where the front splitters and the rear wing would open and close on the press of a button. Reducing the car's drag and increasing top speed.
  • Overall, this car is very good, prioritizes weight reduction by using carbon fiber for most of the components.

What I Learned

Technical and Analytical Takeaways

This case study has massively improved my understanding on the internal components of the car. Especially because they had to strip apart the whole car, I was able to see everything inside and understand how it worked. Including the engine.
